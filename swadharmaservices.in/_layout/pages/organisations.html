Your pañcāṅga, and the welcome line

A tithi is not a global fact. It begins and ends at a moment in time, so which tithi stands at sunrise depends on where you are — and sunrise, rāhukāla, varjya and durmuhūrta are local outright. Two families can owe the same Ābdika on different Gregorian days because they live in different places.

The reckoning is an account property, decided when the account is created — not a platform-wide setting, and not something that drifts afterwards. A family that has kept its Śrāddha by one locality's pañcāṅga for forty years is not moved onto another because a server changed. The demo installation is set to Mysore, India, and this public calendar uses Mysore's tables.

Once the tithi is chosen, everything downstream schedules on the corresponding date — that is what a Purohita's diary runs on — but every booking your devotees see names the tithi first, because that is what they came to arrange.

The welcome line on your own domain

The same date, time and tithi strip that greets people here can sit in your portal's header. One tag and one script, no build step: 

<div data-swadharma-panchanga data-locality="Rajahmundry, Andhra Pradesh"></div>
<script src="https://swadharmaservices.in/panchanga-widget.js" async></script>

data-locality labels the line with your own reckoning. It does not recompute anything — the figures remain the published tables', shown as indicative — so an account that needs its own reckoning needs its own tables. data-href makes the tithi a link; data-theme="dark" suits a dark header. If your portal sets a Content Security Policy, allow swadharmaservices.in in script-src.
